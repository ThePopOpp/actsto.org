§ 01

The Master Mark

A threshold — with a quiet keystone above.

acts·to mark · master

⬤ Concept

An arch is a doorway, a record, a promise.

The mark is a classical archway — two columns supporting a curved lintel on a steady plinth. Above the arch floats a single red dot: the “·” of acts·to, the pivot between obligation and intention, the keystone of every campaign.

Reads as

A school entrance. A church arch. A tax-receipt seal. The threshold a family crosses.

Doesn't read as

A cross. A flag. A logo from a megachurch. A megabank. A clipart cap-and-diploma.

Why the dot is separate

The act of giving lives above the structure, not inside it. The arch holds; the dot decides.

Why navy + red

Civic palette, quieted for paperwork. Red is reserved for the moment of action; navy carries the architecture.

Do not

  • — Recolor the dot to anything other than Liberty Red, navy (campaign red), or 1-color ink.
  • — Rotate the mark. The arch stands upright; the dot rises.
  • — Stretch, skew, or outline-stroke the wordmark.
  • — Set the wordmark in any sans-serif. It is Fraunces or it is wrong.
  • — Drop the “·” from the wordmark. The dot is the brand.
